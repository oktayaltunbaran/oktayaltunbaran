# Backtesting Methodology, Overfitting Prevention and Strategy Validation (for the 2h Supertrend(10,3)+EMA200 crypto perp bot)

Case inputs used throughout: out-of-sample (OOS) Jul 2025-Nov 2026, n = 344 trades, mean +0.24R/trade, profit factor (PF) 1.67, win rate ~34%, 9/10 coins positive; selection over ~30 entry x exit combos (+ a few parameter neighbours) on Nov 2022-Jun 2025. All "own calculation" numbers come from Python scripts (normal approximations + 20,000-path Monte Carlo); they are not quoted from any source.

Research-environment note: WebFetch could not reach en.wikipedia.org, davidhbailey.com, carmamaths.org or people.duke.edu (blocked by session network policy), so primary PDFs of Bailey/Lopez de Prado and Harvey-Liu were NOT read in full; formulas below were cross-checked against search-result snippets and standard references and should be verified against the SSRN papers before being cited in a final document.

## 1. Multiple testing / data snooping (DSR, PBO, White RC, Hansen SPA, HLZ t>3, MinTRL)

### Takeaway
Because the 344-trade OOS period was evaluated once on a single pre-chosen strategy, the multiple-testing penalty applies mainly to the in-sample selection, and the OOS result (t ~2.6-2.8, one-sided p ~0.003-0.005) stays significant even after a Bonferroni-style correction for an effective ~10 trials. It falls just short of the Harvey-Liu-Zhu t>3 bar and of full Bonferroni for 30 trials. If the OOS data was looked at for more than one candidate, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio drops to ~0.77-0.80 for N=30, which is suggestive evidence and not conclusive.

### Cited Findings
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu argue a newly discovered factor needs a t-statistic above ~3.0 rather than 2.0, given the number of factors already tried, and that "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" — [NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592); [SSRN 2513152](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152)
- Harvey & Liu ("Backtesting") turn the Sharpe ratio into a t-ratio, adjust it for the number of tests (Bonferroni, Holm, BHY), and convert back. The "haircut" is the percentage difference. They say a flat 50% rule-of-thumb haircut is "a serious mistake" because the haircut is nonlinear: high Sharpe ratios are penalized moderately and marginal ones heavily. They released code so users can vary the inputs — [CME Group copy of Harvey & Liu "Backtesting"](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf); [Man Group summary](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting); [quantstrat R implementation SharpeRatio.haircut](https://rdrr.io/github/marsanul/quantstrat/man/SharpeRatio.haircut.html)
- The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) corrects for selection bias from multiple testing and for non-normal returns. It tests the observed SR against SR0, the expected maximum SR across N trials when none has skill. SR0 ~ sqrt(V[SR])·[(1-γ)·Φ⁻¹(1-1/N) + γ·Φ⁻¹(1-1/(N·e))], with γ = Euler-Mascheroni 0.5772. DSR = Φ[(SR̂-SR0)·sqrt(T-1) / sqrt(1 - γ3·SR̂ + ((γ4-1)/4)·SR̂²)]. The correction should use the *effective* number of independent trials, not the raw count of configurations — [SSRN 2460551 (Bailey & Lopez de Prado, DSR)](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [endgame-ml implementation notes](https://endgame-ml.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/endgame/utils/sharpe.html); [quant-lessons DSR page](https://5x5x5x5.github.io/quant-lessons/backtest/deflated-sharpe/) (the formulas were reconstructed from snippets and standard references; verify against the SSRN PDF)
- Minimum Track Record Length (MinTRL) is the minimum track length needed for an observed SR to be statistically above a benchmark SR* at confidence 1-α. Formula: MinTRL = 1 + [1 - γ3·SR + ((γ4-1)/4)·SR²]·(z_α/(SR-SR*))². It depends only on the single strategy's properties and not on N — [Wikipedia DSR article (search snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio); [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)
- Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO): Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado & Zhu propose a model-free, nonparametric framework, combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV), to estimate PBO. They argue standard hold-out is unreliable for investment backtests and report that CSCV estimates PBO with small errors — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [Journal of Computational Finance 20(4)](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-computational-finance/2471206/the-probability-of-backtest-overfitting); [eScholarship preprint](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)
- White's Bootstrap Reality Check tests the best of many rules while accounting for dependence across rules. Hansen's SPA test is more powerful and less sensitive to adding poor or irrelevant alternatives. Sullivan, Timmermann & White applied the Reality Check to technical rules on ~100 years of Dow data and found the rules lost predictive power for major US indices after the mid-1980s — [Sullivan, Timmermann & White, FMG DP303](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf); [Park & Irwin (AgEcon)](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/19039/files/cp05pa01.pdf); [Hsu & Kuan RC/SPA re-examination](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/211/Re-Examining%20the%20Profitability%20of%20Technical%20Analysis%20with%20Whitets%20Reality%20Check%20and%20Hansen9s%20SPA%20Tes.pdf)
- Search snippets give conflicting null "expected max SR" examples (100 noise trials of 252 daily returns giving ~0.8 annualized versus 1,000 trials giving 1.0 or 1.59). These depend on the setup, so treat them with caution — [quant-lessons DSR](https://5x5x5x5.github.io/quant-lessons/backtest/deflated-sharpe/); [MQL5 article](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/24150)

### Inferences
- **Basic OOS statistics (own calculation).** SE of mean R = σ/√n. With σ = 1.6: SE = 0.086, t = 2.78, one-sided p = 0.0027, 95% CI [+0.07, +0.41]R, 99% CI [+0.02, +0.46]R. With σ = 1.73 (see section 4 on why σ may be higher): t = 2.57, 95% CI [+0.06, +0.42], and the 99% CI touches 0. With σ = 2.0: t = 2.23, 95% CI [+0.03, +0.45]. The point estimate is weakly identified: the true edge could plausibly be anywhere from ~0.05R to ~0.4R.
- **Bonferroni and Sidak on the OOS t (own calculation).** One-sided critical z is 2.58 for N=10, 2.94 for N=30 and 3.02 for N=40. With t = 2.78, the result passes for an effective N ≈ 10 but fails for N = 30-40. Sidak-adjusted p is 0.027 (N=10), 0.078 (N=30) and 0.10 (N=40). The correction is only needed if the OOS period was used to choose among variants. If OOS was a single confirmatory run of the strategy frozen in-sample, the relevant p is the unadjusted ~0.003-0.005.
- **The ~30 combos are highly correlated.** They share the same coins and period, and many share the entry or the exit, so the effective N is probably closer to 8-15 than 30. This can be estimated by clustering the 30 equity curves by return correlation, which is what Lopez de Prado recommends for N in the DSR.
- **DSR worked example (own calculation).** Per-trade SR = 0.24/1.6 = 0.15, T = 344, null V[SR] ≈ 1/(T-1). SR0 = 0.085 (N=10), 0.112 (N=30), 0.118 (N=40). Using the PF-calibrated distribution (skew ≈ 2.6, kurtosis ≈ 12.5): DSR = 0.93 (N=10), 0.80 (N=30), 0.76 (N=40). With binary-model moments (skew 0.68, kurtosis 1.46): 0.90, 0.77, 0.73. Against the conventional 0.95 threshold, the OOS sample would not pass if it had been used for selection among 30 variants. As a pure single-test confirmation it gives PSR(SR*=0) ≈ 0.998.
- **In-sample selection haircut.** For the in-sample period (unknown trade count, assume 400-800), the expected best-of-30 null per-trade SR is 0.073-0.104. Whatever the winning in-sample SR was, subtract roughly that amount to get the "skill" part. Re-run this with the actual in-sample trade count and SR distribution across the 30 combos.
- **MinTRL (own calculation).** For per-trade SR 0.15 versus SR* = 0, MinTRL ≈ 84-109 trades at 95% and 166-218 trades at 99%, depending on the higher moments. Against SR* = 0.112 (the deflated best-of-30 benchmark), MinTRL = 1 + 0.9·(1.645/0.038)² ≈ 1,700 trades. The data cannot show that this strategy beats "the best of 30 random rules" by a meaningful margin, only that it beats zero.
- **HLZ t>3 bar.** At σ = 1.6 and a true edge of 0.24R, t = 3 needs ~400 trades. At 0.2R it needs ~576, and at σ = 2.0 it needs ~900. Live trading at ~20-25 trades/month (344 trades in ~16.5 months is ~21/month) adds about 250 trades per year. Pooling OOS and live would reach t > 3 in roughly 6-12 months if the edge holds.
- **Practical recommendation.** Run CSCV/PBO on the matrix of the 30 combos (each combo's per-period returns, e.g. weekly or monthly, over Nov 2022-Jun 2025, with S = 8-16 blocks), and run a Hansen SPA test with a stationary bootstrap (e.g. the `arch` Python package, `arch.bootstrap.SPA`) using "flat/no trade" as benchmark. A PBO below ~0.2 and an SPA p below 0.05 would make the selection step defensible. A PBO above 0.5 means the best in-sample combo is no better than a median one OOS.

### Gaps
- Primary PDFs (DSR, PBO, Harvey-Liu "Backtesting") could not be fetched because of the network block, so the exact formula notation and the authors' numeric haircut tables were not checked.
- No source was found giving a recommended PBO acceptance threshold. The 0.2 figure above is my own heuristic.
- The in-sample trade count, the in-sample SR of each combo, and whether OOS was ever looked at for more than one candidate are unknown and drive the result.

## 2. Walk-forward, CPCV, Monte Carlo reshuffling / bootstrap, drawdown and risk of ruin

### Takeaway
A single hold-out (what was done) is one path. Walk-forward also gives a single path. CPCV and CSCV give a distribution of OOS outcomes and a PBO estimate. Monte Carlo on the calibrated trade distribution says to expect a ~11R median and ~19R 95th-percentile max drawdown over 344 trades, and ~14R at the 95th percentile over a 100-trade window. Because positions on 9-10 correlated coins overlap, real drawdowns will be larger than these iid figures, so use date-block bootstrap. Fixed-dollar risk ruin probability is very sensitive to the true edge.

### Cited Findings
- Purged cross-validation (Lopez de Prado, 2017) prevents look-ahead leakage. CPCV builds many train-test splits, purges overlapping samples and adds an embargo. Conventional and walk-forward backtests can be over-optimistic because of leakage and overfitting — [Wikipedia: Purged cross-validation (search snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)
- Walk-forward produces only a single backtest path, which makes overfitting hard to assess. CPCV gives many paths, a distribution of Sharpe ratios and a PBO computation — [Time-series CV visual tutorial](https://pjalgotrader.github.io/Interactive_tools/m5_ml/ts-cross-validation-visualization.html) (tutorial, not peer-reviewed)
- An SSRN paper (id 4778909, via a Scribd copy) reports that CPCV beats K-fold, purged K-fold and especially walk-forward on PBO and on the DSR test statistic, and that walk-forward shows notable shortcomings in false-discovery prevention. The authors and publication status were not verified — [Scribd copy of SSRN 4778909](https://www.scribd.com/document/725401650/SSRN-id4778909)
- Anchored walk-forward fixes the training start and expands the window forward. Reusing the same inner validation set many times can itself cause overfitting — [MQL5: Unified Validation Pipeline Against Backtest Overfitting](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/21603)

### Inferences
- **Calibrated trade distribution (own calculation).** PF = 1.67 and mean +0.24R imply gross wins ≈ 0.598R and gross losses ≈ 0.358R per trade. With a 34% win rate, the average winner is ≈ +1.76R and the average loser only ≈ -0.54R, because the Supertrend trailing exit closes many losers before the full stop. A model with losers ~U(-1.05, -0.05) and winners 0.05 + Exp(1.71) reproduces mean 0.24, PF 1.65 and σ ≈ 1.50 (skew 2.6, kurtosis 12.5). σ ≈ 1.5-1.6 is therefore consistent with the reported PF. Compute the actual σ from the 344 trades rather than assuming it.
- **Monte Carlo (iid trade resampling, 20,000 paths, own calculation).**

  | Horizon | Max DD p50 | p90 | p95 | p99 | Longest losing streak p50 / p95 / p99 | P(total R < 0) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 50 trades | 5.5R | 9.4R | 10.9R | 13.9R | 7 / 13 / 17 | 13% |
  | 100 trades | 7.2R | 12.0R | 13.8R | 18.1R | 9 / 15 / 19 | 5% |
  | 344 trades | 11.0R | 16.9R | 19.2R | 24.4R | 12 / 18 / 22 | 0.1% |
  | 100 trades, true edge 0.12R | 10.5R | 18.0R | 20.8R | 26.1R | – | 23% |
  | 100 trades, true edge 0 | 15.2R | 26.0R | 29.6R | 36.6R | – | 54% |
  | 344 trades, binary model (-1R / +2.65R) | 16.8R | 26.1R | 29.9R | 38.9R | 12 / 18 / 22 | 0.5% |

  The binary-model row shows how much drawdowns depend on the loss-size distribution. Use the empirical trades.
- **Use block bootstrap, not iid reshuffling.** Trades on 9-10 large caps open at the same time in the same direction, since crypto correlations are high, so losses cluster. Resample whole calendar weeks (stationary or block bootstrap) of portfolio P&L. This typically fattens the drawdown tail relative to the iid table above. Also report the drawdown in account % at the planned risk, including concurrent exposure (e.g. 6 open positions × 1% = 6% at risk at once).
- **Walk-forward for this bot.** With so few parameters (ATR period 10, multiplier 3, EMA 200), parameter re-optimization adds little. The more useful exercise is a rolling-origin check: freeze the rule and compute expectancy per 6-month block and per coin, then check the sign and stability of each. Also run a parameter-plateau check (ATR 8-14 × multiplier 2.5-3.5 × EMA 150-250). A robust strategy shows a smooth, positive surface. A spike at (10, 3, 200) indicates overfit.
- **CSCV/PBO implementation.** Build a T×N matrix (T = weekly returns ~135 weeks for Nov 2022-Jun 2025, N = 30 combos), split into S = 10 or 12 blocks, form all C(S, S/2) combinations (252 for S = 10, 924 for S = 12), find the IS-best combo in each, record its OOS rank, and take PBO = share of combinations where the OOS rank falls below the median.
- **Risk of ruin, fixed-dollar risk (Brownian approximation P(ruin) ≈ exp(-2μB/σ²), B = ruin buffer in R, own calculation).** With μ = 0.24, σ = 1.73: B = 10R gives 20%, 20R gives 4.1%, 30R gives 0.8%, 50R gives 0.03%. With μ = 0.12 (half the backtest edge): 45%, 20%, 9%, 1.8%. With μ = 0.07: 63%, 39%, 25%, 10%. With zero edge, the probability of hitting -20R within 344 trades is ≈ 53%, and of -10R ≈ 76%. For a tiny account with a fixed $ risk, the account should hold at least 30-50R of risk units. For example, a $1,000 account risking $20/trade has only a 50R buffer if a full wipeout is acceptable. A tolerable stop-out point (e.g. a 30% loss) translates to 15R at $20, which is too small. Risk ≤ 0.5-1% of starting equity per trade.
- **Fixed-fractional vs fixed-dollar.** Fixed-fractional risk (risk f% of current equity) never reaches zero mathematically, but it slows recovery after losses and shrinks bets in drawdown. Fixed-dollar risk recovers faster but can be ruined. For small accounts with exchange minimum notional limits, fixed-dollar risk with a hard stop-trading floor (e.g. stop at -20R) is simple and auditable.

### Gaps
- No primary source on Pardo's walk-forward efficiency ratio thresholds could be retrieved. The commonly quoted "WFE > 50-60%" heuristic is unverified and omitted from the findings.
- No crypto-specific study comparing iid and block-bootstrap drawdowns was found.

## 3. Biases: survivorship, look-ahead, repainting, intrabar fills, funding, liquidity/slippage

### Takeaway
The biggest unmodeled items are (a) survivorship and selection of the coin universe from coins that exist and are large today, and (b) funding costs. Funding at the Binance baseline of 0.01%/8h on a multi-day long costs ~0.03%/day, which for a 3-5% stop is ~0.01-0.03R per trade per 3-day hold at baseline and several times that in bull markets. Look-ahead and repainting risk for Supertrend/EMA on closed bars is low if signals are taken at the next bar's open. Verify it mechanically with a truncation test like Freqtrade's lookahead-analysis.

### Cited Findings
- A University of St. Gallen study (2022) quantifies how survivorship and delisting bias distort crypto factor performance. In a survivor-only sample the size premium is overstated by about 50%, and after controlling for the bias the momentum, beta and downside-risk relationships tested show no positive link to returns — [alexandria.unisg.ch](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/handle/20.500.14171/108037)
- Grobys, Sandretto & Äijö, "On survivor cryptocurrency momentum", *Finance Research Letters* 92 (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.frl.2026.109602, argue that significant documented momentum payoffs are an artefact of coins that are only temporarily accessible for trading (paper not opened; summary source) — [securities.io summary](https://www.securities.io/why-crypto-momentum-backtests-can-mislead-investors/)
- Survivorship-bias-free practice is to include every instrument that met the eligibility rules at the time, even if it later delisted. Survivorship-biased backtests tend to overstate returns, understate drawdowns and overestimate liquidity — [CoinAPI blog](https://www.coinapi.io/blog/how-to-eliminate-survivorship-bias-in-crypto-backtesting) (vendor source)
- Binance perpetual funding is settled every 8 hours (00:00, 08:00, 16:00 UTC). The interest component is fixed at 0.03%/day (0.01% per interval). The BTCUSDT cap/floor is ±0.3% — [Binance FAQ: Introduction to Funding Rates](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/360033525031)
- Since May 2025, when a USDⓈ-M contract's previous settlement hits the cap or floor, Binance switches its settlement frequency to hourly — [Binance announcement 2025-05-02](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/3243c81a35bd4f0c86a37315c3dc96cc)
- Annualization: an 8-hour rate × 3 × 365, so 0.01% ≈ 10.95% APR. Third-party figures put BTC funding averages at ~109% annualized in Feb 2024 and ~5% in May 2024 (unverified), and an OI-weighted BTC average of 0.0054%/8h ≈ 6% annualized in a Sep 2026 snapshot — [spark.money funding comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/funding-rate-comparison); [DeFi Rate BTC perp](https://defirate.com/perp/bitcoin/)
- Freqtrade's `lookahead-analysis` re-runs the backtest on truncated data and flags changed indicator values or moved entries and exits. It can only verify trades it actually re-checked, so a clean result can be a false negative. `recursive-analysis` checks indicators whose values depend on how much history is loaded (e.g. EMA warm-up) — [Freqtrade lookahead-analysis docs](https://codeclimate.com/github/freqtrade/freqtrade/docs/lookahead-analysis.md/source); [Freqtrade recursive-analysis docs](https://codeclimate.com/github/freqtrade/freqtrade/docs/recursive-analysis.md/source)

### Inferences
- **Survivorship for this universe.** The bot trades 9-10 large caps chosen today. That carries survivorship risk in the universe choice (coins that stayed top-10 through 2022-2026 had above-average trends) more than delisting risk. Test: rebuild the universe point-in-time, e.g. the top-10 USDT perps by trailing 90-day volume at each quarter start, including coins that later fell out (LUNA and FTT in 2022, and other mid-caps), and rerun. Binance data.binance.vision keeps archives of delisted symbols' klines, so check whether those files exist for the dropped coins.
- **EMA200 recursive warm-up.** On 2h bars, EMA200 needs about 3-5× its length (600-1,000 bars ≈ 50-80 days) before values converge. Make sure backtest and live use the same warm-up, and that the first trades of each coin are not taken on unconverged values.
- **Repainting.** Supertrend and EMA computed on closed bars do not repaint. Repainting happens only if signals are evaluated on the forming bar or if higher-timeframe data is joined without a shift. The rule: signal at bar t close, fill at bar t+1 open (plus slippage). Test it by running the backtest on data truncated at random points and checking that the earlier signals are identical.
- **Intrabar assumptions.** "Stop before target in the same bar" is conservative. Also check: (i) gaps through the stop (the fill should be at the bar's open or worse, not at the stop price); (ii) exchange stop-market execution during wicks; (iii) a 1-minute data replay for a sample of trades to measure how far the 2h-bar approximation is off.
- **Funding cost per trade (own calculation, assumptions labeled).** Assume an average hold of 3 days, a stop distance of 4% of price, and longs paying the baseline 0.01%/8h: funding = 9 × 0.01% = 0.09% of notional ≈ 0.0225R. In a hot market (0.03-0.05%/8h for altcoins in a rally) this is 0.07-0.11R per long trade. Shorts usually receive funding when it is positive, so the net effect depends on the long/short mix. Compare the modeled round-trip cost of 2 × (0.06% + 0.05%) = 0.22% ≈ 0.055R at a 4% stop. Funding could add 40-200% on top of modeled costs for longs. Download historical funding from data.binance.vision (the `fundingRate` monthly files) and charge it per 8h boundary crossed.
- **Slippage at small size.** For 9-10 large caps at retail notional (< $5k), the market-impact share of slippage is negligible. The 0.05% assumption is mainly for latency and spread. Measure it live: log the signal price versus the fill price for every trade, and compare against the 0.05% assumption after ~50 trades.

### Gaps
- No Binance-specific historical average funding for 2023-2026 per coin was retrieved. It is needed for a real estimate.
- No study quantifying survivorship bias specifically for large-cap-only trend following was found. The St. Gallen and Grobys results concern cross-sectional factors across broad universes.
- The actual average holding period and stop distance (in %) of the bot are unknown. The funding figures above depend on them.

## 4. Statistics: trades needed, CIs, losing streaks, Kelly and risk-of-ruin

### Takeaway
To tell a +0.2R edge from zero at the conventional 5% level with 80% power takes ~500 trades at σ = 1.6, and ~800 at σ = 2.0. The 344 OOS trades are enough to say "probably positive" but not enough to pin down the size. Expect ~12 consecutive losers over 344 trades (p95 ≈ 18). Full Kelly on the backtest numbers is ~8-9% risk per trade. Using quarter-Kelly on a shrunk edge gives ~1% risk per trade, before adjusting for concurrent correlated positions.

### Cited Findings
- The HLZ t>3 threshold and the Harvey-Liu t-ratio transformation (SR → t = SR·√T) used below — [SSRN 2513152](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152); [CME copy of Harvey & Liu "Backtesting"](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf)
- MinTRL formula and its independence from N — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [quant-lessons DSR](https://5x5x5x5.github.io/quant-lessons/backtest/deflated-sharpe/)

### Inferences
(Everything in this section is my own calculation from standard formulas: SE = σ/√n, n_req = ((z_α + z_β)·σ/μ)², longest-run approximation ≈ ln(n·p_win)/ln(1/p_loss), continuous-Kelly f* = μ/σ², binary Kelly f* = p - q/b.)
- **Standard error and CI.** SE = σ/√n = 1.6/√344 = 0.086R. The 95% CI for expectancy is 0.24 ± 1.96 × 0.086 = [0.07, 0.41]R.
- **σ floor.** If every loss were exactly -1R, a 34% win rate with mean 0.24 needs average wins of 2.65R and gives σ = √(pq)·(W+1) = 1.73. The actual PF of 1.67 implies smaller average losses (≈ -0.54R) and σ ≈ 1.5. Compute σ from the trade log.
- **Trades required to detect an edge:**

  | True edge | σ | n for t = 2 | n for t = 3 | n for 80% power (α = 5%, two-sided) | n for 80% power (α = 0.05/30, one-sided) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0.24R | 1.6 | 178 | 400 | 349 | 634 |
  | 0.20R | 1.6 | 256 | 576 | 502 | 913 |
  | 0.20R | 2.0 | 400 | 900 | 785 | 1,426 |
  | 0.10R | 1.6 | 1,024 | 2,304 | 2,009 | 3,652 |
  | 0.10R | 2.0 | 1,600 | 3,600 | 3,140 | 5,706 |

- **Losing streaks.** With P(loss) = 0.66, the expected longest losing run is ≈ 6.8 over 50 trades, 8.5 over 100, 10.2 over 200 and 11.5 over 344. Monte Carlo agrees: median 12, p95 18, p99 22 over 344 trades, and median 9, p95 15 over 100 trades. A run of 15 losers in the first 100 live trades is within normal variation. Pre-commit to that so it does not trigger a discretionary shutdown.
- **Kelly.** Full Kelly on backtest numbers: μ/σ² = 0.24/1.6² = 9.4% (0.08 at σ = 1.73). Binary Kelly is p - q/b = 0.34 - 0.66/2.65 = 9.1%. Shrink the edge first: taking half the OOS mean as the planning edge (0.12R) gives full Kelly ≈ 4.0%, half-Kelly 2.0% and quarter-Kelly 1.0%. With up to 9-10 concurrent and highly correlated positions, Kelly applies to the portfolio's aggregate risk, so divide per-trade risk by an effective number of independent bets. If 6 open positions behave like ~2 independent bets, each position's risk should be ~1/3 of the single-bet figure. Practical result: 0.3-0.5% of equity per trade at launch, with a cap on total open risk (e.g. ≤ 3%).
- **Risk of ruin formulas.** For fixed-dollar risk with unit R: P(ruin before reaching infinity) ≈ exp(-2μB/σ²), where B is the account size in R units. Worked numbers are in section 2. For fixed-fractional risk f with log-growth g ≈ f·μ - f²σ²/2 per trade, the probability of ever touching a drawdown D (as a fraction) is approximately (1-D)^(2μ/(fσ²) - 1) in the Brownian approximation. At μ = 0.12, σ = 1.6, f = 1%: exponent = 2 × 0.12/(0.01 × 2.56) - 1 ≈ 8.4, so P(ever hitting -50%) ≈ 0.5^8.4 ≈ 0.3% and P(ever hitting -25%) ≈ 0.75^8.4 ≈ 9%. (This is my derivation from standard diffusion results, not checked against a source.)

### Gaps
- No primary source for Van Tharp's R-multiple / SQN or Ralph Vince's optimal-f formulas was retrieved. The formulas above are standard, but not cited from those authors.
- The actual per-trade R distribution (σ, skew, autocorrelation) and the concurrency of positions are unknown. They change all the numbers here.

## 5. Live deployment monitoring, kill switch, incubation and ramp-up

### Takeaway
Pre-register monitoring rules before going live. Kill or pause if the drawdown exceeds the Monte-Carlo 95th-99th percentile for the elapsed trade count, or if the live mean R is significantly below the backtest mean. Note that a 100-trade window has weak power: a 14R drawdown threshold (p95 for a true 0.24R edge) catches a zero-edge strategy only ~58% of the time. Incubate for ~50-100 trades at minimum size, then ramp risk in steps tied to trade counts.

### Cited Findings
- Survivorship-biased backtests overestimate liquidity and understate drawdowns, so live drawdowns are expected to exceed backtest drawdowns — [CoinAPI](https://www.coinapi.io/blog/how-to-eliminate-survivorship-bias-in-crypto-backtesting)
- Coinbase Institutional (Sep 2022) reported crypto trend following underperformed in all but two of the previous 7 quarters while cumulative returns stayed positive, and stressed its sensitivity to timing and entry/exit levels. This shows multi-quarter dry spells are normal for the style — [Coinbase Institutional, Trend following in uncertain times](https://www.coinbase.com/institutional/research-insights/research/monthly-outlook/trend-following-in-uncertain-times-september-2022)

### Inferences
- **Kill-switch power (own Monte Carlo, 100-trade window, threshold = 13.9R, the p95 max drawdown under a true 0.24R edge).** P(trigger) is 4.9% if the true edge is 0.24R (the false-alarm rate), 25% at 0.12R, 58% at 0R and 82% at -0.10R. A drawdown-only kill switch is a safety net against disaster, not a quick test of whether the edge exists.
- **Live mean versus backtest (own calculation).** Even with a true edge of 0.24R, the live mean is negative after 30 trades 20% of the time, after 50 trades 13%, after 100 trades 5% and after 150 trades 2%. Do not judge before ~100 trades.
- **Suggested pre-registered rule set:**
  1. **Hard stop (capital protection).** Pause if the live drawdown exceeds the p99 for the elapsed trade count from the block-bootstrap MC (≈ 18R at 100 trades and ≈ 24R at 344 using iid figures; recompute with block bootstrap), or the account loss limit in R from section 2, whichever comes first.
  2. **Review trigger.** Investigate if the drawdown exceeds p95 (≈ 14R at 100 trades) or the losing streak exceeds p99 (≈ 19-22).
  3. **Statistical decay test.** After each batch of 50 trades, compute a one-sided t-test of live mean R against the backtest mean 0.24 (H0: μ_live = 0.24). A sustained t < -2 means the live process differs from the backtest. Also run a CUSUM or sequential probability ratio test (SPRT) of μ = 0.24 against μ = 0.
  4. **Execution drift.** Track slippage (fill versus signal), funding paid per trade, and missed or duplicated signals. Compare per-trade R of live trades with the same trades re-simulated by the backtester on the same dates ("shadow backtest"). A gap of more than ~0.05R/trade points to implementation error, not market change.
  5. **Distribution checks.** Compare live win rate (expect 34% ± ~4.7 pp at n = 100), average win, average loss and holding time with the backtest using two-sample KS or Mann-Whitney tests every 100 trades.
- **Incubation and ramp-up.** (a) Paper trading or a testnet for 2-4 weeks mainly validates plumbing (orders, stops, reconnection) and cannot validate the edge (~20-40 trades). (b) Live at minimum size (≤ 0.25% risk or exchange minimum notional) for the first ~100 trades (≈ 4-5 months at ~21/month). (c) Increase to 0.5% if live expectancy is within the backtest 95% CI and execution drift is < 0.05R. (d) Go to the target 0.75-1% only after ~250+ live trades, when pooled OOS plus live data reaches t ≥ 3. Cut size back a step whenever the review trigger fires.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source on recommended incubation lengths for systematic retail strategies was retrieved. The schedule above is derived from the power calculations and has no cited basis.
- No source on SPRT or CUSUM application to trading-strategy monitoring was retrieved.

## 6. Regime change and strategy decay

### Takeaway
Published anomalies decay substantially after publication: the McLean & Pontiff working paper found 58% lower long-short returns post-publication, implying ~32% attributable to publication after removing ~26% statistical bias. Technical-rule profitability in US indices disappeared after the mid-1980s. Crypto time-series momentum still shows up in recent studies, but its profits are concentrated in bull markets and very sensitive to the sample. Plan for the live edge to be substantially below +0.24R, roughly 0.1-0.15R.

### Cited Findings
- McLean & Pontiff study 97 characteristics shown to predict cross-sectional returns (*Journal of Finance* 2016, 71(1):5-32) and conclude investors learn about mispricing from academic publications — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:71:y:2016:i:1:p:5-32); [published PDF copy](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)
- An earlier working version (82 characteristics) reports that the average predictor's long-short return shrinks 58% after publication. Combined with an estimated 26% statistical bias, this implies a lower bound of ~32% for the publication effect. Press coverage cites ~35% decay. The published version's headline figures differ from the 58% draft figure — [Ivey working paper PDF](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf); [Counterpoint Funds copy](https://counterpointfunds.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/PredictabilityMcleanPontiff.pdf); [Chronicle of Higher Education](https://www.chronicle.com/blogs/percolator/academic-research-destroys-stock-values)
- Technical trading rules lost predictive power for major US indices after the mid-1980s (Reality Check study) — [Sullivan, Timmermann & White, DP303](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf)
- A recent crypto study with realistic assumptions finds strong time-series momentum, with profits concentrated in bullish markets — [Springer, Financial Markets and Portfolio Management (s11408-025-00474-9)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)
- Crypto momentum results may be artifacts of tail events in particular samples, and crypto momentum is subject to severe crashes, which makes results sample-period-sensitive — [AUT ACFR paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf); [Wisselink thesis, EUR](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/44390/Wisselink-NJ-483391-BA-thesis.pdf)
- Crypto trend following underperformed in 5 of 7 quarters to Sep 2022, with cumulative returns still positive — [Coinbase Institutional](https://www.coinbase.com/institutional/research-insights/research/monthly-outlook/trend-following-in-uncertain-times-september-2022)

### Inferences
- **Haircut chain for planning.** OOS +0.24R × (1 - ~30-50% decay/overfit haircut, based on the McLean-Pontiff magnitude and Harvey-Liu's argument that marginal t-stats deserve heavy haircuts) gives a planning expectancy of ≈ 0.12-0.17R. Then subtract unmodeled funding (≈ 0.02-0.05R for longs) to get ≈ 0.08-0.15R. At that edge, ~1,000-2,500 trades are needed to confirm statistically, so live monitoring must be framed around capital protection and implementation fidelity, not around proving the edge quickly.
- **Regime dependence to check now.** Split OOS by BTC regime (e.g. BTC above or below its 200-day MA, realized-volatility terciles) and by long versus short. If most of the +0.24R comes from one rally leg (e.g. a single quarter or a single coin), effective independent evidence is much smaller than 344 trades. Also check 9/10 coins positive against what correlation would give for free: with correlated coins and a market-wide trend, 9/10 positive is less informative than it would be for independent assets.
- **What to monitor live.** Rolling 50- and 100-trade expectancy versus the MC band, share of trades exiting at the initial stop, average winner size (trend following lives on the right tail, so a shrinking average win is the first sign of decay), BTC realized volatility regime, funding-rate level, and correlation among the 10 coins (higher correlation means fewer independent bets and larger drawdowns).

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly measures secular decay of crypto trend-following profitability with rolling sub-periods. Evidence is mixed and sample-dependent.
- The published McLean & Pontiff figures could not be confirmed from the abstract snippets. The 58% figure is from the working-paper version.
